Building the European Knwoledge Graph Infrastructure for Science, Businesses and Public Sector

In the evolving multipolar world, Science faces unprecedented challenges from within as well as from outside. At the same time the European idea manifested in the politics of the European Union are in a crisis, partially for the same reasons.
To see good answers how both can be met, one has to step back, to see the broader context:
There are too many indicators to deny, that the USofA have become tired of their global political and socio-cultural leadership.  They had once taken up the idea of a just and free humanity informed by universalist science and have been leading in many fields, most of all in the realm of the digital revolution. A revolution that has massively transformed not only the economy and the ways we live and communicate, but also science itself. And as much as one can refer to Marshall McLuhans famous formula "The medium it the message" one might say, the message of the digital medium is inherent US American. The expertise developed in this field enabled the US to maintain economic world leadership inspite of the decline of traditional industries. They were the first to devlop profitable business models building on the new digital paradigm, winning (back) operational segments like pharma or mobility. Now, it seems they are at the onset of a new, second digital revolution, which is coined the "AI" revolution. The resources the US economy is investing in that field are not to underestimate: It is more than 400 Mrd. US $ in 2025. "Last quarter, spending on artificial intelligence {in the US} added more to GDP than total consumer spending.”[^1]
It is quite obvious, that those spendings are incited by the opportunity to outpace contenders and profit even more from those investments, than by those expenditures incurred during the first digital revolution. So while AI is the buzzword of those times, we need to remember, that it is coined by companies, that  invest into it as their product. And while it is very unclear, what artificial intelligence can mean in that respect (that of products), one has to be aware, that beginning with the term itself, the surrounding claims are first of all marketing jargon.
Looking at the efforts undertaken in the US, from an European perspective one could contend, that the case is already lost for Europe. How could we catch up, given the massive infrastructure US companies already invested in building infrastructures for so called AI technologies?
Now, there is a chance. Surprisingly it emanates from science, that is informed one might want to say by good old European values. It is a path, that turns a alledged weakness of Europe into a strength. We're talking of the cultural richness of Europe, that was a drawback in competition with the uniform consumer needs and habits that ruled the US market. 
Now what are we talking about?
If we look at the resent computer technologies labeled as AI, we are talking of models, simulating neuronal networks, that can perform for tasks, they previously have been trained for. And it is true that the US are leading in the infrastructures for and the delivery of those models. But to be applicable to real world problems (economic or scientific) one needs validated data. Nobody understands more of data Validation than science. And there is a new technology too, which is a kind of revolution itself: the Knowledge Graph technology.
Secretly this revolution already lurks behind the powerful movement that is evolving in Europe to improve research data management. It is advocating the FAIR-Principles. This acronym means: Findable, Accessible, Interoperble and Reuseable. Not everybody in the field is aware, however, that this acronym was coined to promote Knowledge Graph Technology. To be sure it was meant to formulate principles, that are useful for science and businesses likewise. E.g. to be accessible is a feature that can be shared by commercial data as opposed to open.
Knowledge Graph or semantic web techniques are also called semantic AI as opposed to neural-network learning (as in LLMs).
